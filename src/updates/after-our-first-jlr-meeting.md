<p class="lead">Our first meeting with JLR’s UK Director of Client Care and Head of Legal for Supply Chain was courteous and constructive. Both appeared genuinely concerned about members’ experiences and keen to understand the evidence our community has assembled.</p>

JLR told us it is particularly keen to repair the customer experience. It recognised that this has
been inconsistent and, too often, poor. It wants existing Jaguar owners — including those who bought
their I-PACE used — to know that the brand has not moved on from them. That is welcome. Delivery now
matters.

The meeting allowed us to explain our letter directly, put members’ experiences behind the headline
numbers and ensure senior JLR leaders understood why owners need a proper resolution.

## What we heard — and what remains unanswered

JLR showed real interest in the data and customer experiences we are collecting. However, it offered
no substantive update on H570, H571 or H572. A programme offering full high-voltage battery
replacement, as other manufacturers have provided when facing comparable EV battery problems, does
not appear to be imminent.

Our member survey was clear: [Full HV Replacement won](/updates/september-survey-results/), with 681
selections and 420 preferred votes from 847 responses. A Fair Buy Back also received strong support.
JLR now needs to respond with a credible route forward for affected owners.

## JLR asked about access to member data

JLR asked for more of our evidence and customer experiences, particularly VINs, so it can match
cases to individual vehicles. It cited disconnects among retailers, dealer groups, Jaguar, JLR and
Tata to explain why nobody has a complete view. We do not believe that fully accounts for the gap.
Authorised dealerships use TOPIx to record work and support invoicing, so JLR should already be able
to analyse much richer source data than the subset members record with us.

We also challenged JLR over members’ reports that approaches to its direct customer support channels
went unanswered or were dismissed. Whatever the internal boundaries, owners should not have to
reconstruct JLR’s own case record.

**We do not currently have members’ permission to share their records, VINs or experiences with
JLR, and we will not do so.** If JLR makes a useful, properly safeguarded proposal, we will explain
it to members first and ask each person for explicit consent. Only data from members who choose to
take part would be shared.

Any proposal would need a clear purpose, appropriate privacy and security safeguards, defined
access and retention arrangements, and a credible explanation of how sharing the data would help
affected owners.

## The commitment we now need

JLR said it will send minutes based on notes taken by its Head of Legal for Supply Chain. Our
substantive ask is one clear commitment: **a board-level statement to I-PACE owners setting out how
JLR will resolve the high-voltage battery issue to the satisfaction of affected owners.**

We asked JLR to provide a written statement that we can share with members **by the end of next
week**, before the launch of its new car. Owners have endured recalls, restrictions, repeat visits
and uncertainty for long enough. A prompt, substantive response is now needed.

## What happens next

We want this introduction to become a productive relationship and lead quickly to the fair,
dependable resolution owners have asked for. After the deadline, we will publish JLR’s response or
report that none was received.

If the response does not satisfy members, the group will consider its next steps, including legal
avenues.

## Can you help?

Thank you to everyone who has recently volunteered to help. We believe we now have sufficient legal
and litigation experience for the current stage, although more can only help. We would be particularly
interested to hear from members with media or public relations experience, or relevant press and
media contacts.
